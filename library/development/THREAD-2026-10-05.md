## 2. The critic passes — what they found

The passes ran on the model-layer document, one after another, each attacking a fresh surface. The
count in the document is **eight passes and sixteen changes**, and the loop **converged**: the tenth
pass found nothing new.

**The sharpest finding was a "fix" that was itself wrong.** The seventh pass called a sentence in the
Sprint-0 runbook a "wrong document", on the stated grounds that the runbook contains no occurrence of
the word "embeddings". **Both halves were false.** The runbook *did* record the phrase at line 161,
and the pass could not find it because the same change (commit `111171a`) had rewritten that entry.
Two routes disagreed — `git blame` said the line was added by `111171a`, `git show 111171a~1` said
"embedding" already existed before it — and that disagreement is what exposed the error. The lesson:
**re-measure against the tree the claim was made about, not the tree you are standing in.**

Also corrected: a stale carrier (the CHANGELOG still said "two passes / seven claims"), a wrong
count in the lessons (three claims credited to "the vision" when it was two), and several precision
gaps so a re-runner lands correctly (the `lib/` path base, the location of `hermes_cli/providers.py`,
the `context_policy.py` line numbers, the `_llamacpp_pdef()` line). Every remaining claim was
re-measured clean.

**Nothing was defended. Every finding was corrected in the text, and the correction is visible.**

## 4. The two requirements for the open-source lane

Both are the owner's, both are recorded where the design will meet them.

**(a) The open-source lane must not reserve a vendor's settings.** Written into the model-layer
document **§4.2**. The rule is general to every provider, not just llama.cpp: adding a backend must
not require borrowing another vendor's environment variable names. Concretely, the local lane gets
its own `LLAMACPP_API_KEY` / `LLAMACPP_BASE_URL`, and the same discipline applies to every supplier
we add.

**(b) The platform must manage which local model runs.** Written into the model-layer document as a
**new §6.4, "Managing the local model from the platform"**. Four pieces: a deployment model record, a
hardware profile, a **growable catalog** (data, not code, so a new model release is an entry not a
rebuild), and a **platform-driven swap**. A swap is a restart, not a hot reload, and that is stated
plainly.

**The Hermes mapping intent** is recorded in [`AGENT-HALF.md`](AGENT-HALF.md) §5: a Hermes **persona**
is a `SOUL.md` identity block, and a Hermes **profile** is `hermes -p <name>`, an isolated runtime
home. Mapping agents, personas and profiles onto tenants and roles is now a planned item, because the
model-default decision gives it a reason.

**Two folders the design cites were sitting untracked in the working tree:**

  `BLOCKERS.md` and the CHANGELOG.
- `analysis/` — the earlier Hermes / Starting Kit study, five findings plus a forensic baseline.

**Both are ours, small, and not rebuildable**, so a clone without them had broken citations. They are
now tracked (2026-10-05). A third folder, `hermes-agent-main/` at the root, is the **Hermes runtime
source** (somebody else's code, about 17,000 files); it is now ignored, matching its twin
`library/ai/hermes-agent-main/`. The full honest list is [`BOOKMARKS.md`](BOOKMARKS.md) §5.

**This is an our-call, not an owner decision.** It is reversible: `git rm -r --cached analysis llama`
un-stages them.
